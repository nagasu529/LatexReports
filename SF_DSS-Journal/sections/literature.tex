This section reviews relevant literature in three areas: farmer heterogeneity in agricultural development \cite{feder1985,anderson2004}, machine learning applications in agriculture, and explainable artificial intelligence for decision support systems.

\subsection{Farmer Heterogeneity and Training Effectiveness}

A long-standing insight in agricultural economics is that farmers are far from homogeneous. Variations in human capital, resource endowments, and access to information shape both productivity and technology adoption. Consequently, standardized training programs often deliver uneven outcomes across participants.

Empirical studies consistently show that farmers with stronger baseline skills tend to gain more from advanced training, while those starting with lower capabilities may find it difficult to absorb complex content. Training effectiveness is therefore closely tied to individual characteristics \cite{davis2012}, underscoring the importance of tailoring interventions rather than relying on uniform approaches.

Despite this understanding, many extension systems continue to implement one-size-fits-all programs, largely due to operational constraints and the absence of tools that support systematic targeting. This disconnect between evidence and practice highlights the need for data-driven methods that can guide more adaptive training allocation.

\subsection{Machine Learning in Agriculture}

Machine learning has gained substantial traction in the agricultural domain in recent years \cite{kamilaris2018,liakos2018}. Applications now span crop yield prediction, disease detection, and resource optimization. Ensemble models such as Random Forest and gradient boosting are particularly prominent, owing to their strong performance on structured agricultural datasets.

These models excel at capturing non-linear relationships and interactions—features that are common in agricultural systems. Compared with traditional regression techniques, machine learning approaches generally offer stronger predictive accuracy, especially when dealing with high-dimensional or complex data.

Recent work has also shown the potential of AI-driven systems in automating operational tasks. For example, AI-based quality inspection and classification systems have been found to improve accuracy and consistency relative to manual processes \cite{wonggasem2024}. Such developments illustrate how AI is increasingly influencing both analytical and operational aspects of agriculture.

Nevertheless, most existing studies remain focused on production and environmental outcomes, with relatively limited attention to human capital development and training systems. Moreover, many machine learning applications emphasize prediction without addressing how model outputs can be translated into actionable decisions, which limits their practical usefulness \cite{ryo2022}.

\subsection{Explainable AI and Decision Support Systems}

As machine learning models become more complex, interest in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) has grown accordingly \cite{arrieta2020,lipton2018}. Among the available techniques, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) has emerged as a widely used approach for interpreting tree-based models due to its theoretical grounding and its ability to provide both global and local explanations \cite{lundberg2017}.

SHAP decomposes model predictions into feature-level contributions, enabling users to understand not only which variables matter, but also how they influence outcomes. This level of transparency is particularly valuable in decision support contexts, where interpretability is often a prerequisite for adoption.

Despite these advances, the integration of XAI into operational decision-making remains limited. Many studies focus on interpretation alone, without offering a clear pathway for converting model explanations into structured decision rules. This study addresses that gap by linking SHAP analysis with segmentation and rule extraction, enabling the development of a decision support system that is both practical and deployable \cite{yang2025,zhang2026}.
