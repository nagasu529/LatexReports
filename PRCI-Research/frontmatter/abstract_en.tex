\chapter*{Abstract}

This study evaluates the impact of participation in the Smart Farmer program on farmers’ skill development using data from participants and non-participants during 2022–2023. Econometric and machine learning approaches are combined. Propensity Score Matching (PSM) constructs comparable groups, and Difference-in-Differences (DID) estimates causal effects while addressing selection bias.

Results show that participants have higher skill levels than non-participants both before and after the program. However, DID estimates indicate no statistically significant improvement attributable to participation.

Machine learning and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) identify key determinants of skill outcomes, including baseline skills, technological capability, education, and infrastructure such as irrigation. The findings also reveal substantial heterogeneity in treatment effects across farmers.

These results suggest that uniform program design may limit effectiveness. Targeted interventions—such as tailoring training to initial skill levels, strengthening digital skills, and improving infrastructure—are recommended to enhance long-term outcomes.

\textbf{Keywords:} Smart Farmer, Farmer Skills, Impact Evaluation, Propensity Score Matching, Machine Learning
